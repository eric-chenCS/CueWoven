# Using CueWoven

[Install first](../INSTALL.md), then open the search popup either way: press **Ctrl+Shift+F** (**⌘+Shift+K** on Mac), or click the CueWoven toolbar icon (pin it from Chrome's puzzle-piece menu to keep it visible). Chrome skips the shortcut if another extension already uses it; Settings shows the current one and **Change** opens Chrome's shortcut page. Your library can start empty or with optional original sample videos.

## Search what you remember

Describe an idea, phrase, image or scene, then press **Enter**. Typing alone does not run a search or show matches. Editing the query hides old matches until you submit again. Each search is independent. After a search, **Not it? Add a detail** below the results adds one thing you remember (something said, something on screen, a channel or site, or when), and it opens on its own when nothing matches. Details build up as tags under the search box; ✕ removes one and searches again. **Not this one** on a result hides that item and searches again; the "hidden" tag brings it back. Editing the search clears its details and hidden items. Source/date filters narrow the results: **Any time**, **Today**, **7 days**, **30 days** or **Custom**, which shows From/To dates for an exact range. You can also type when you saw something: "3 days ago", "3-4 days ago", "between 1 and 2 months ago", "a few weeks ago", or, with words like "I watched", "yesterday", "last 3 days" or "in September". Typed dates are padded because memory is fuzzy, appear under the search box as "Using: …", and can be turned off with **Ignore suggested filters**; if nothing matches, results without them are shown. A date chosen in the popup always wins over a typed one. Results show the best match as a larger card. **Other matches** below it opens up to three more videos or pages as compact rows, one row each; it starts folded when the best match is confident and opens by itself when the top two nearly tie (the second row says **Almost as good**) or when the best match is only related in meaning. **Show more** reveals more rows and loads further ranked matches as needed. ↑ and ↓ move through all results, and Enter opens the selected one, or the best match.

Naming a source explicitly, such as “a YouTube video about back pain” or “an article from nature.com about coral”, searches saved items from that website first. The applied website appears below the search box. If none match, CueWoven says which website had no matches; **Search without suggested filters** broadens the search when you choose it. Automatic date relaxation keeps the website restriction. A topic such as “an article about YouTube” does not select a website.

A moment is a saved passage or observation at a video time. Indicators identify the evidence: Transcript, Audio, Scene, On-screen text, Article image, Image caption or Title. Title-only results open a source without claiming a matching spoken timestamp. Relevant text and speech matches take priority over image similarity. Scenes and saved screenshots are used as a fallback when no strong text match is found; weak incidental text does not prevent visual searches. Scores express ranking, not probability.

On a supported video page, **Search this video** limits results to the current video. Saved speech, on-screen text and scenes can be searched even when captions are unavailable or new capture is paused. If no moment evidence has been saved yet, CueWoven first attempts to capture the current video; it cannot retrieve speech or scenes that were never saved. Uncheck the option to search the rest of your library again.

Press Enter once to search; press Enter again to open the shown result, or use **Copy link**.

When a search has no matches, CueWoven asks you to **Add one more detail**. Weak or closely tied results also open the optional detail controls while keeping the matches available. Follow-up questions use your search and any details you already added: a car search can ask what the car was doing, while a speaker search asks about remembered words. **Something said**, **On screen**, **Channel or site** and **When** switch between questions about that topic. These use local templates; unfamiliar topics get a question using the search terms. Questions do not infer answers from results or add text to your search. Short searches are allowed, and details are added only when you type them and press Enter.

Queries containing only punctuation or common English filler words (such as `...`, `that`, `or`, or `I`) show **Describe something you remember** without submitting a search or checking/indexing the current video. Meaningful single words such as `car`, `Minecraft`, `open`, short names and numbers remain searchable. Double quotes deliberately allow an otherwise filtered word, such as `"I"` or `“that”`. This submission check does not disable normal model warmup when the popup opens.

Opening the popup prepares text search without preloading visual search. Visual models load when capture or an actual visual search needs them. The text worker unloads after a minute without work; downloaded model files stay cached on disk. This reduces idle memory at the cost of a short reload after a break. Initial downloads and slower devices can take longer. [Resource behavior and measured limits](PERF.md#current-model-residency).

If the text engine repeatedly stops, CueWoven allows one immediate replacement, then limits new workers to two starts per minute to prevent rapid restarts from accumulating memory. Further attempts show a retry countdown; try again after that interval. Healthy searches keep reusing their worker without this wait. This safeguard does not guarantee recovery from a native browser-process crash.

For article results, a literal phrase found in the saved passage becomes the highlighted jump target, even when it occurs later in that passage. Surrounding saved words help distinguish repeated phrases. Similar-meaning results scroll near the saved passage without highlighting its words and show **Suggested section · Similar in meaning**. The label disappears on click, typing, keyboard navigation, touch or scrolling; interacting before a late-rendered passage appears also cancels the pending jump. If an exact target has disappeared or cannot be distinguished on the current page, CueWoven reports that it could not find the saved passage. Generated image captions open the page without claiming an on-page text location.

## Choose what gets saved

Saving is enabled with YouTube as the only starter site. On first run, the welcome screen offers the suggested sites as three groups (News, Reference, Social), all off; choosing any and pressing **Start saving** asks the browser once for just those sites. Add them later, or your own domains, under Websites. Browser access is requested on a user click; already granted access may not show a new prompt. Refresh existing tabs afterward.

The × beside a site excludes future capture while keeping saved items searchable. The popup's **Forget site** action deletes saved items for that site and blocks future capture. The site list and extra options can be collapsed. Broader public-website access is optional.

Allowed pages contribute up to 2,000 visible words and up to three accessible article images. Compatible visible video players can contribute native captions, played speech, scene snapshots and on-screen OCR. Website audio transcription is separately off by default. Native social-media apps, inaccessible frames, protected media and login walls are outside coverage. Feed URLs may reopen a feed rather than an exact clip.

Speech fallback is local and English-only. It starts automatically when captions are unavailable; no Save now or speech-start button is needed. Missing/undetermined language metadata permits an English attempt, which can be inaccurate if the actual audio is not English. Known non-English audio is skipped. Pause saving or use **Settings → Transcribe when there are no captions** to control it; Lite mode also pauses speech. It processes only audio played after starting, one player at a time. Audio is not saved as a recording.

Visual capture samples frames; it does not continuously archive a video. Short events and small or stylized text may be missed. **Lite mode** pauses new article images, scenes, OCR and speech while keeping captions and article text. Settings also provide individual controls, light/dark/system appearance and **Reset settings to default**. Reset preserves the library, site choices and retention preferences.

**Save visual details is off by default.** This single Settings switch controls video scene snapshots and article-image capture, which share the visual model and can use substantial RAM and CPU. Previously saved visuals remain searchable when it is off. Captions, text search, OCR and speech fallback are unchanged. Existing separate choices are preserved until you toggle the combined control; a mixed state is explained beside it. Resetting Settings turns both off.

The first time automatic Lite setup runs (including an update from a version without it), devices reporting 8 GB of physical RAM or less switch to Lite mode. Larger devices keep Lite mode off by default, with scene snapshots still requiring opt-in. This uses the browser's `system.memory` permission locally, without uploading memory information or polling free RAM. Settings explains the device default; turn Lite mode off to resume the capture features you enabled. Your choice is preserved across restarts and updates. **Reset settings to default** restores the device-based default. If memory detection is unavailable, the current Lite choice is kept, or the normal Off default is used for a fresh install; detection is retried on a later service-worker start. Previously saved media stays searchable, so Lite mode does not impose a fixed browser-memory limit.

## Videos saved without a transcript

CueWoven saves videos as you watch them; there is no link pasting, Takeout importer or browser-history scan. A video saved as a title only, or with AI speech for just the parts you played, tries its captions again the next time you watch it. Older title-only entries stay searchable by title, and old CueWoven/Recall/Momento backups still restore.

## Privacy and data

Your library, vectors and settings are stored in the current browser profile. Local models process search text, audio and images on your device. Model weights download from Hugging Face on first use; normal website/YouTube requests and thumbnails still use the network. No personal YouTube API key is required.

Site allowlists, exclusions, incognito avoidance, sensitive-route filtering and text redaction reduce unwanted capture. Known authentication services such as Duo and Okta, SSO/MFA routes, authentication-token URLs and visible one-time-code fields are excluded. Public university pages and articles about security remain eligible; `.edu` and the word “security” alone do not make a page private. These checks cannot identify every signed-in/private page, and captured images are not redacted. Grant access only to sites you want to save; pause capture when appropriate. Diagnostics show approximate storage and a bounded session-only domain/reason log rather than full page content.

Under **Your data**, export/import backups, clear the library, or forget items saved recently. One JSON backup contains saved transcripts, page passages, titles, source links, timestamps, OCR text and captured images. Treat it as private and do not commit it to the repo. It does not contain original video files, recorded audio, complete websites, settings, site permissions or downloaded models.

Uninstalling the extension or clearing its browser profile can erase the library. There is no account or automatic cloud sync. Export before switching profiles/computers or uninstalling, then restore into the new installation. A source that disappears cannot be brought back online by the backup; only previously saved evidence can be restored. Keep settings open during restore; stop speech before restoring. Completed batches remain if a restore is interrupted.

If processing reports an interruption, check the library before retrying. Searches can recreate a closed processing document; writes with uncertain completion are not automatically repeated. If Edge disables CueWoven after a renderer crash, reloading the extension or restarting Edge may be necessary.

Retention choices are 24 hours, 7 days, 4 weeks, 12 months or Forever. Watched/read content uses the applicable recent capture/watch date. Existing legacy imported records retain their original protection and stored retention preference for compatibility; the old import setting is no longer shown. Watching such an item makes normal live retention apply. **Forget recent** uses when CueWoven saved/updated an item. Export before deleting if you need a copy.

**Image storage** under Your data bounds scenes and article images to 120 MB, 240 MB (default, up to 20,000 images), 500 MB or 1 GB of accounted compressed-image/vector payload, with the image count scaled to match and 240 images per video. Larger sizes keep more images but load more search data into memory for each image search, about 40 MB at the default and 170 MB at 1 GB. Lowering the size asks first, then removes the oldest images that no longer fit. Images otherwise follow **Forget what I watch or read after**; if the size is reached first, the oldest images are removed early and Your data says so. Full web content has a separate 2,000-item or 64 MiB accounted payload cap, with older items retained as titles. Models, browser storage overhead and other evidence use additional space. This is a searchable memory aid, not a complete archive.
